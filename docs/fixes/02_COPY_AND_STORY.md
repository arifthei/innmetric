# Copy decisions and the page story

The aim is a capable person speaking to another capable person. Keep the operator tone in Cursor's draft. Remove the parts that sound like an internal procedure or a performance of certainty.

## The proposed homepage opening

H1, retained by Mert:

> The desk can quote the room. The guest still cannot buy it.

Lede:

> Your team shouldn't have to chase the same booking problem through every system.

Supporting copy:

> InnMetric investigates room availability and rate problems in the systems your hotel already uses. We make the changes you approve and check what guests can book.

Primary CTA remains `Let's Solve It!`. The secondary link remains `See how we work`. The short helper invites the problem and wanted outcome, with a clear reply on whether the work fits. It does not repeat access and approval instructions already explained on the process and contact pages.

This is an editorial proposal under the latest brief. It does not turn the lede into another permanent lock.

## Give each page a useful job

| Page | Reader's question | Content that earns its space | Useful next route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Is this the problem we have, and what changes if we bring you in? | Recognisable booking problem, short service explanation, useful handover, three engagement choices and fit | How it works, Services or Contact |
| How it works | What will my team have to do? | Three phases, each with a short Your team / InnMetric split. What happens when an issue stays unresolved | Services or Contact |
| Services | Which work do we need? | Buyer state once per offer, what is included and clear boundaries | Contact |
| About | Who will actually do the work? | Founders and practices the hotel can check, without invented support anecdotes | Contact |
| FAQ | Does this cover the question I searched? | Six direct answers with the real scope limit. No mandatory sales pitch at the end of each answer | Relevant answer or Contact |
| Contact | What should I tell you, and what happens after I do? | Decision-maker form, current non-sending state, working email route and the reply sequence | Email while preview remains non-sending |

A visitor may enter through any page or go straight to Contact. The story supplies enough context on each page; it does not force a linear navigation path. Consistent navigation is useful and should not be varied to satisfy a humanizer pattern.

## Changes that matter

| Current wording | Proposed direction | What it preserves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "One party owns the trace" | "We make the changes you approve and check what guests can book." | Responsibility and hotel approval |
| "Four things your team holds" | "Know what changed and what still needs attention." | Findings, approved changes, repeat checks and handover, scoped to the agreed work |
| "You want it to stay fixed" | "You need to know when a problem returns." | The purpose of rechecks without permanence claims |
| "Tell us the booking problem you want owned" | "What needs to work better at your hotel?" with availability/rate context immediately below | Decision-maker audience and wanted outcome |
| "We believe ... So we ..." repeated | A specific belief as the heading, followed by the practice it produces | The source's belief-to-action relationship |
| "What should be true when it is fixed" | "What is happening and what would you like to change?" | Useful problem and outcome in the same field |

Keep the four wheel titles, the three offer names, the responsibility split and the six FAQ questions. Preserve the latest founder-card layout. Shortening text is the first layout intervention; changing global padding or shrinking type to fit redundant prose is not.

## Voice checks

Read one passage as a hotel manager receiving a colleague's explanation. Does it name the problem and say something useful? Can you replace an abstract noun with the actual task? If a sentence only repeats the heading or the previous paragraph, cut it. Keep the detail that changes the buying decision.

Use contractions when natural. Avoid making every line equally short or every section end in a slogan. "Human" does not require deliberate mistakes, swearing, invented emotions or jokes. Necessary terms such as PMS, stop-sell and room mapping can stay where they explain the work. Do not put the entire systems list in every introduction.

Use the writing references in two passes: first the wording, then the page outline. The second pass asks what is repeated and whether the page answers a new reader question. Preserve necessary approvals and genuine uncertainty; move them to the place where they matter.
